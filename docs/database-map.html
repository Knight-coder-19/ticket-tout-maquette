The Eighteen Tables of CartePro

The schema does not describe tables, it lays down rules. This map shows how the tables hang off one another and, for each, the guarantee it carries — the one that would be lost if it were removed.

users · employees · employers · employment_links · partners → accounts

First pivot: everyone ends up on an account

Two paths lead to accounts, one per kind of actor, and they do not look alike. A merchant owns their account outright, from registration. A member of staff does not: it is their employment link that carries one, which is what allows it to be closed when they change employer without erasing their history.

ledger_operations · ledger_entries · payments · topups · compensations

Second pivot: nothing moves without an operation

Every sum that moves creates one row in ledger_operations and exactly two in ledger_entries. Everything else — a payment, a top-up, a reversal — is only a supporting document hung off that operation: it says who, why and under what circumstances, never how much.

ledger_entries.prev_hash · ledger_entries.hash

What makes tampering detectable

Entries are not merely forbidden to change: they seal one another. Each carries the digest of the one before it, and its own digest is computed over its fields plus that one.

the eighteen tables, by what they guarantee

What each table carries

Grouped not by feature but by regime: what may be modified freely, what may never be modified, and what gets marked instead of deleted.

reference data and identity — modifiable, never deleted

cities

The register of towns, populated by migration 0002. Deliberately closed: if the town were free text, "Lyon" and "lyon" would be two towns and the catalogue filter would never return anything trustworthy.

users

The sign-in account: email, password digest, role, status. The email is CITEXT, so unique regardless of case — otherwise two accounts could exist for the same person.

one role per user

employers

The administrations that fund their staff's cards. Kept apart from users because an employer is not someone who signs in: it is a legal entity, and its access goes through api_clients.

employees

The civil identity of a member of staff — surname, first name, phone. Distinct from users because that information outlives the sign-in account and must not be duplicated if someone changes email address.

employment_links

The link between a member of staff and an administration: their staff number there, and the account that goes with it. This is where changing employer plays out — the old link moves to ended, a new one is born, and the history stays whole.

one active link per person staff number unique per employer, among active links

partners

The merchant: legal name, trading name, category, review status, town and service mode. An online business has no town, a physical one must have one — otherwise it would appear in no filter and be invisible forever.

physical ⇒ town required reviewed ⇒ reviewer and date recorded

partner_highlights

The features chosen by the ministry, with their placement and position. Removing one records removed_at and never deletes the row: who put which shop in the window, and when, is exactly what an audit can ask for.

one shop once per placement one position once per placement

sessions

Open sessions. The token is hashed here; the clear value lives only in the browser cookie, so a stolen database opens nobody's session. Revocation records a date, it does not erase the row.

api_clients

Machine credentials for the administrations' HR systems. Kept apart from human users because they authenticate differently — no cookie, a hashed secret — and are rate-limited differently.

the money — the account, then the ledger

accounts

The pivot of the system. balance_settled is the balance, balance_held is what tokens in circulation have reserved, and the available amount is the difference. The stored balance is a cache: the ledger remains the truth, and recompute_balance exists to prove the two agree.

balance ≥ 0 except system accounts held ≥ 0 held ≤ balance

ledger_operations

One movement of money: its kind, its amount, when it happened and when it was recorded. The two dates are distinct because a payment taken offline is scanned at one moment and transmitted at another.

amount strictly positive append-only

ledger_entries

Two rows per operation, a debit and a credit of the same amount, chained by digest. This is the table that makes the system auditable: we do not ask the code to be honest, we prove it by replaying the chain.

digests exactly 32 bytes digest unique append-only

the supporting documents — primary key = operation_id

payment_tokens

The promise of payment, before it becomes an operation: amount, fallback short code, expiry, status. It is the only table that exists between the two moments of a payment, and it is its amount that balance_held immobilises meanwhile.

expiry after issue one active short code at a time resolved ⇒ resolution date

payments

The settlement: which token, which merchant, which entry mode, when scanned and when synchronised. The uniqueness of token_jti is the protection against double settlement — and it holds even if two merchants scan the same QR code at the same instant.

a token settled only once

topups

The top-up: which employer, which account credited, which payroll reference. The money comes from the MINISTRY_ISSUANCE system account, whose negative balance measures the total issued.

topup_batches

The payroll file that was uploaded, with its digest. The digest is what refuses a duplicate upload of the same file — the most useful protection in a bulk import, because re-uploading a file by mistake is the easiest thing in the world to do.

one file once per employer

compensations

The reversal: the operation that corrects another, with the reason and the administrator who approved it. In an immutable ledger you do not cancel an error, you post its inverse and keep both traces.

an operation cannot compensate itself

the trail

audit_log

Who did what, when, from which address. Append-only like the ledger: these are the two tables that cannot be rewritten, one for the money, one for administrative decisions.

append-only

Immutability is laid down in two layers, and that is deliberate. The application role cartepro_app is denied the UPDATE, DELETE and TRUNCATE privileges on the three append-only tables. The database owner, however, bypasses privileges — hence the second layer, the forbid_mutation() function and its triggers, which raise an exception whoever issued the statement.

That is what test I8 checks: it connects as the owner, attempts a direct UPDATE, and expects an error.
